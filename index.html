01 / WHO WE ARE

One industry.
One shared voice.

Barbering is a skilled craft, a local business and a source of connection in communities across Ireland.

The Irish Barbers Federation exists to help the industry build its future together. We are bringing barbers, educators and industry partners into a conversation about professional standards, learning, representation and the value of the craft.

The IBF is a voluntary, independent, industry-led body. It does not regulate the trade or control who may work as a barber.

One profession. One federation. A higher standard together.

Barbering is a skilled profession built on education, experience, creativity and trust. The IBF Professional Barber Licence is being developed to recognise barbers who meet defined requirements and commit to the federation’s professional standards.

The licence is a voluntary IBF credential, not a statutory or Government licence. It is in development and cannot yet be applied for or paid for.

WHAT IT IS, AND WHAT IT IS NOT

Clear about
what it certifies.

What the licence is

  • A voluntary professional credential issued by the IBF;
  • Open to IBF members who satisfy published eligibility and assessment requirements;
  • Renewable every year.

What the licence is not

  • Not a statutory licence or a legal requirement to work as a barber in Ireland;
  • Not Government authorisation, accreditation or recognition;
  • Not insurance, and not proof of insurance cover;
  • Not a guarantee of competence;
  • Not available simply by paying: payment alone never guarantees a licence.

Barbers who do not hold an IBF licence remain free to practise. The IBF does not regulate the trade.

HOW IT IS INTENDED TO WORK

A fair,
clear process.

Proposed, not yet approved

This is the process the IBF is working towards. It is subject to IBF approval and may change before launch.

  1. Choose Licensed Professional Membership. Open to IBF members.
  2. Submit your evidence. Evidence of identity, training or experience, and acceptance of a code of conduct. A fair route for experienced barbers without formal qualifications is intended.
  3. Assessment. An IBF assessor reviews your application and may ask for more information.
  4. Decision. You receive a reasoned approval or refusal, with a fair route to ask for a review.
  5. Issue. On approval and any applicable payment, a digital licence is issued.
  6. Renew. The licence is maintained and renewed every year.

Eligibility requirements, assessment arrangements and fees will be published before applications open.

QUESTIONS

Licence FAQ.

Is the licence compulsory?

No. It is voluntary. The IBF does not regulate the trade or control who may work as a barber.

Is it a Government licence?

No. It is an IBF professional credential. It is not a statutory licence and it is not Government authorisation or recognition.

Can I get it by paying for it?

No. A licence is issued only when the published requirements are met. Payment alone never guarantees a licence.

What will it cost?

The fee has not been confirmed. Standard Membership is €190 a year as part of the IBF 2000 campaign offer.

What will I need to show?

The requirements are not yet published. We intend to include a fair route for experienced barbers who do not hold formal qualifications.

Does the licence include insurance?

No. Insurance is separate from licensing. The IBF is exploring insurance partnerships, and any partner will be named only once a written agreement is in place.

Can clients check my licence?

Online verification of licence status is planned for when the licence launches. It is not available yet.

When will it launch?

We do not have a launch date. We will publish the criteria and the process before applications open.

05 / STANDARDS & EDUCATION

Raising the bar,
together.

Barbering depends on skill, hygiene and trust. The IBF is working towards a shared, voluntary approach to professional standards and development.

Standards & certification Foundational work

We want to help shape a shared approach to professional standards and recognition across the industry. The IBF Professional Barber Licence is intended to be one part of this. It is voluntary, and its criteria are not yet published. Areas such as hygiene, safe working practice, professional conduct and continuing learning are being considered, and nothing is final.

Any scheme must be fair to experienced barbers who do not hold a particular certificate, and must not create unjustified barriers to working in the trade.

Training & development In development

We want to champion learning, practical skills and pathways that help barbers progress throughout their careers. We intend to talk to educators, colleges and training providers, and to explore how our work can sit alongside existing Irish qualifications and apprenticeships.

HOW WE APPROACH IT

How we
work, openly.

The IBF is building a constructive voice for the industry.

  • We speak for the barbers who join us, so a larger and more organised membership means a stronger case;
  • We base our positions on evidence, and we will not make national claims about the industry until they can be properly verified;
  • We are voluntary, independent and industry-led. We do not regulate the trade, and we do not claim Government endorsement.
